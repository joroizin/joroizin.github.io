THE VERDICT

The single biggest finding: gifmania.app is not in Google at all. site:gifmania.app returns nothing — before any keyword strategy matters, the site needs Search Console verification and an indexing push. Bonus problem: Google currently thinks "gifmania" means old GIF-clipart portals (gifmania.com and friends), so even the brand query needs repair.

The opportunity is real and unusually specific. Three separate research tracks converged on the same shape: the searches GIF Mania can win are the ones where tiny indie sites already rank — and none of them has a product as finished as yours. Nobody ranking for gif wars is a playable game (it's all "how to run it manually in Slack" articles). The free jackbox alternative SERP is held by one-person projects. The party long tail (party games no app download, browser party games free) has almost no major media in it. Meanwhile the big generic team-building keywords are a content fortress (Asana, Deel, teambuilding.com) — we don't attack those directly; we get GIF Mania featured in their listicles instead.

06TRADEMARK: "KAHOOT MEETS CARDS AGAINST HUMANITY"

Researched against real enforcement history, not just first principles. Short version: the phrase is fine — keep using it.

  • Nominative fair use protects naming a competitor to describe your product ("alternative to X", "X meets Y") as long as it's accurate, uses no more of the mark than needed, and doesn't imply endorsement. The entire alternatives industry runs on this — Mentimeter, Wooclap and a dozen others operate dedicated anti-Kahoot landing pages unmolested, and Jackbox itself publishes a "games like Cards Against Humanity" listicle.
  • What CAH actually enforces: lookalike products — the black-and-white card trade dress and confusable names ("Crabs Adjust Humidity" lost at the TTAB; "Humanity Hates Trump" got C&D'd). Never comparative mentions.
  • What Kahoot enforces: logo/wordmark styling. No case found against "Kahoot alternative" content.
  • House rules: plain-text names only (no logos, no card-style visuals); accurate comparisons; a small footer disclaimer on comparison pages ("Kahoot! and Cards Against Humanity are trademarks of their respective owners; GIF Mania is not affiliated"); never in the domain or product name. Bonus: "Kahoot meets Cards Against Humanity" is an essentially unclaimed phrase in Google — using it consistently means owning it.

07THE MEASUREMENT LOOP

This is what makes the process continuous instead of a one-shot bet. Two data sources, one monthly ritual.

Search Console = demand side

The only source of query → impressions/CTR/position data (PostHog can never see queries — Google strips them). It answers: which keywords are we starting to appear for, and which pages under-convert their impressions? Bing WMT adds exact volume numbers and the AI-search ecosystem.

PostHog = outcome side

Already wired. Define one funnel per landing page: organic landing → game created → game started → 4+ players joined. It answers: which keywords bring people who actually host games? Join the two by landing-page URL in a simple monthly spreadsheet — no paid connector needed at this scale.

The monthly 30 minutes

  1. GSC Queries report: find terms sitting at position 5–20 with impressions — those are the "almost ranking" pages; improving them beats writing new ones.
  2. PostHog funnels: which landing pages turn visitors into hosts? A keyword that ranks but doesn't convert gets demoted; one that converts gets a better page.
  3. Decide one action: improve an almost-ranking page, ship the next planned page, or do one outreach batch. One action per month is the sustainable cadence.
  4. Quarterly: refresh dates/content on comparison pages (freshness visibly matters in these SERPs) and re-rank the keyword map with real volume data.
