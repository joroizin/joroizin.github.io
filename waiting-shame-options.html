“Everyone is waiting for Maya” — shame options

Five takes on calling out the last player still picking a GIF, on the host screen and /tv during the submitting phase. Mock data: 8 players, 7 locked in, ~12s left on a 45s clock. Each stage is the real 1280×720 submitting layout with the shame element added — hit Replay to re-run the entrance.

Behavior & plumbing notes (all options)
  • The host today only receives submitted_count — the shame needs the backend to broadcast the names of pending players (or just the last one) on submission progress and in the snapshot.
  • Must respect presence: the pending set should be waited-for players only — never shame someone who disconnected (the 60s stop-waiting rule already excludes them from early-advance).
  • Copy needs an en + he catalog entry; the name sits mid-sentence, so both languages need their own template (Hebrew flows RTL).
  • Question: trigger as soon as exactly one player remains, or only when the clock is low (say ≤20s)? Instant is funnier; delayed is kinder to fast rooms.
  • Question: also shame the last voter during voting, or submitting only?
  • Question: when two stragglers remain — stay silent, or “Everyone is waiting for Maya & Omer”? (Mocks show the single-name case.)
  • Question: should the laggard’s own phone get a nudge too (vibration + “everyone’s waiting for you!”)? Phones stay silent audio-wise per the product rule; vibration is available.

Option A — Callout pill

The submitting screen stays exactly as it is; a wobbling acid pill drops in below the counter. Same visual grammar as the warm-up / final-round banners, so it costs nothing new.

+ Minimal, on-brand, trivially reversible; the screen stays about the question. − Easy to miss from the couch; the least “moment”-like of the five.

Option B — Spotlight takeover

The whole stage dims and the laggard’s name goes up in lights — giant display type under a spotlight, countdown ring front and center. The room will chant the name. Reverts the moment they submit.

+ Maximum party energy; unmissable; a real game-show beat. − Hides the question and progress; strongest shame — could feel harsh for shy players or kids’ games (could gate it off when kid-friendly is on).

Option C — Roster flip

When one player remains, the anonymous “7 / 8” counter flips into the full roster: everyone who locked in gets a mint ✓ chip, the straggler’s chip burns magenta and wobbles. Shames by contrast — and gives the finished players their little moment too.

+ Informative, celebrates the fast players, gentle-but-clear shame; scales to the two-stragglers case for free. − Busiest layout of the five; chips get tight at 20 players.

Option D — Breaking-news ticker

A “LIVE” news crawl slides up from the bottom edge and loops. The stage above is untouched, but the motion makes it impossible to ignore. The crawl copy can rotate through a few jokes.

+ Funny, non-blocking, very TV; extra joke lines are free content. − Continuous motion competes with the countdown; jokes need writing (and translating) well.

Option E — Wanted poster

A tilted WANTED poster swings in beside the counter: mystery mugshot, the name, “last seen browsing trending GIFs”. Pure Jackbox energy — a prop, not a banner.

+ The most characterful; screenshots well; shame lands as a joke about the situation, not the person. − Most bespoke CSS to build/maintain; needs the counter nudged aside to make room.
